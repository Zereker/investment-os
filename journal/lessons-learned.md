# Investment Lessons Learned

This journal records durable lessons rather than temporary market opinions.

## Lesson 1 — Returns came primarily from holding

The strongest historical results came from holding high-conviction assets such as MU, TSM, GOOG, and SPY rather than from frequent trading.

**Policy implication:** holding is the default; trading must prove its necessity.

## Lesson 2 — Out-of-formation trades created avoidable losses

Past losses were more common in names outside the established capability circle and portfolio formation.

**Policy implication:** research first; do not trade unfamiliar assets.

## Lesson 3 — Cash improves both resilience and behavior

A meaningful cash position provides liquidity, prevents forced decisions, and improves emotional stability during drawdowns.

**Policy implication:** protect the 30% warning threshold and treat approximately 35% as the operating target.

## Lesson 4 — Concentration should decline through incremental diversification

Selling a winner solely to reduce concentration can create tax and opportunity costs. Directing new capital toward broad-market ETFs can gradually reduce portfolio dependence on a single stock.

**Policy implication:** prefer incremental drift toward SPY/VOO over forced rebalancing, unless fundamental or risk conditions justify a sale.

## Lesson 5 — Price is a trigger, not a verdict

Technical levels and price changes are useful prompts for analysis but should not directly determine long-term decisions.

**Policy implication:** price alerts analysis; fundamentals decide; discipline executes.

## Lesson 6 — A second opinion blocks impulsive trades

Requiring a written or external review creates useful friction before execution.

**Policy implication:** every active trade must pass the documented review process.
