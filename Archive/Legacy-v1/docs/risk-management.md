# Risk Management Framework

## 1. Objective

Risk management exists to protect the ability to compound over the long term. The priority is survival, consistency, and decision quality—not maximizing short-term return.

## 2. Cash Policy

Cash is a strategic position, not idle capital.

- Target cash level: approximately 35%
- Warning threshold: 30%
- Danger zone: below 30%
- When cash falls below the warning threshold, restoring liquidity takes priority over expanding risk exposure.

## 3. Concentration Risk

Single-stock and industry concentration must be monitored explicitly.

- Existing winners are not reduced solely because they appreciated.
- Concentration should preferably decline through new capital flowing into broad-market ETFs.
- Increasing an already concentrated position requires a full strategy review.

## 4. Leverage

Default policy: no leverage.

Any future use of leverage would require a formal Investment OS revision rather than an ad hoc exception.

## 5. Price Risk vs. Thesis Risk

Price movement alone is not a decision rule.

- Price triggers analysis.
- Fundamentals determine the decision.
- Discipline determines execution.

## 6. Sell Triggers

A sale may be considered when at least one of the following applies:

1. The fundamental thesis materially deteriorates.
2. The original thesis is disproven.
3. Portfolio risk becomes materially higher than planned.
4. A clearly superior long-term opportunity exists and passes full review.

Fixed price levels belong in snapshots, not in this document.

## 7. Risk Radar

Each cruise review should assess:

- Cash risk
- Single-stock concentration
- Industry concentration
- Valuation risk
- Fundamental risk
- Execution-discipline risk

The objective is to detect rising risk before it becomes a forced decision.
